     01             Document overview, sources and source conflicts


1.1 Purpose
VidhyaSaathy is a school-connected digital learning workspace. Each school class gets an online workspace that
mirrors the offline class, so that the school's support continues after the school bell rings. This document turns the
team's sources into one working specification that a designer, developer, school stakeholder or new team member
can read without further explanation.


1.2 Sources reviewed
S1 Founder's voice-note transcripts (Hindi and Hinglish). Translated in this document.
S2 Founder's written master prompt on the product idea and research goals.
S3 "Team Guide" PDF, team draft v1 dated 13 October 2026. It marks each item as CONFIRMED, PROPOSED or
OPEN.
S4 Master working spec with feature numbers VS-01 to VS-100 (the "Krazy features" list), including AI concepts,
teacher tools, parent tools and India-specific layers.
S5 Product Vision and Feature document (long-form PRD style), with sections on student, teacher, parent and
school workflows.
S6 Brand and theme image: logo, cloud mascot, palette, mascot expressions, a sample poster and a sample phone
home screen.
S7 Earlier summary and feature-list PDF prepared for the team in English.
Not supplied: any working prototype, screenshots of a built app, school data, user research results, or technical test
results. Nothing in this document is evidence that the product works.


1.3 Source conflicts the team must resolve
C1 Product name. Team Guide says "The name is not final" and the mockup is titled ClassOS. All other sources use
VidhyaSaathy. Decision: 15 (Section 30).
C2 Tagline. The logo shows "Learn . Grow . Together". Earlier drafts proposed "Grow at your own pace." Only one
tagline should appear publicly. Decision: 16.
C3 Student login. S3 says a roll number alone is not secure. S4 describes a roll-number search, a school-issued
activation code and a parent-phone OTP. Direction in this document: the roll number identifies the student but never
logs them in. Method is [OPEN].
C4 Technology stack. S3 lists options (React or Next.js, or Flutter;
Node.js or Django; MongoDB or PostgreSQL). S4 names Supabase with PostgreSQL, TypeScript and Tailwind.
Decision: 14 (Section 30).
C5 Performance bands. S3 and S5 call them "four internal bands". S4 assigns them to a labelled "Student Progress"
view in one place. This document follows S3: bands are never shown to students as labels.
C6 Session completion. S3 proposes that a session is complete only when a quiz or check is attempted. S4
suggests slides viewed or quiz passed.
Direction: a quiz or check attempt is required. Decision: 8.
C7 Teacher-controlled automation. S4 includes AI generation of questions, lessons and a predicted-question feature
(AI-40). S2 and S5 require teacher approval. This document applies teacher approval everywhere AI content can
reach students.

1.4 Conventions used in this document
• "Student", "teacher", "parent" and "school admin" mean the roles in Section 05.
• "Class workspace" means the online space for one class (for example Class 9-A).

• "Evidence" means a recorded learning action (for example, a quiz answer).
• "Mastery estimate" is a working estimate from evidence. It is never presented as a proven fact.

     02             Product identity and philosophy


2.1 What VidhyaSaathy is
• A school-connected learning workspace for each class.
• A bridge between classroom teaching and home study.
• A place where teachers publish approved notes, recordings, sessions and homework.
• A place where students follow a clear next step after school.
• An AI helper that answers questions in the context of the school's own material.
• A progress view for teachers (class level) and parents (own child).


2.2 What VidhyaSaathy is not
• It is not a generic AI chatbot. The AI works inside the school's context.
• It is not a video library. YouTube videos are supporting material only.
• It is not a file-sharing app or a group chat.
• It is not a full school ERP. Transport, fees and attendance are out of scope unless the team approves them later.
• It does not replace teachers. Teachers approve content and make final assessment decisions.
• It does not claim to remove the need for coaching. The long-term goal is to make school support stronger; the
  effect is not yet proven.


2.3 Founder motivation
The founder was a strong student who scored above 90% in earlier classes.
In Class 9, studying became chaotic. The founder did not attend coaching, searched YouTube, asked friends for
notes (and received conflicting versions), and used AI tools without a clear path. This is one student's experience. It
is a reason to investigate the problem, not proof that every student faces it.


2.4 Product philosophy (ten principles)
P1 School remains central. The syllabus, teachers and approved resources form the base of the experience.
P2 AI supports teachers and does not replace them.
P3 Personalisation follows learning evidence, not labels. Students are never called weak, average or intelligent.
P4 Understanding matters more than activity. Opening slides is not learning.
P5 Parents receive useful insight, not only marks and not private chats.
P6 The product must work for real Indian school conditions: shared devices, several languages, variable connectivity
and limited teacher time.
P7 The experience is calm and encouraging. No public shame-based rankings, no manipulative streaks, no
excessive notifications.
P8 AI output must be grounded and reviewable. Important content should trace back to school material or another
identified source.
P9 Privacy is a product requirement, not an afterthought.
P10 Every major feature connects to the learning loop: identify a need, take an action, check whether the action
worked.

     03             The core learning loop

The whole product is built around one loop. Every feature in this document serves one or more steps.
 1. Teacher teaches the lesson in class.
 2. Teacher publishes notes, recording and a guided session to the class workspace (LES-01, LES-05, SES-01).
 3. Student studies the session and attempts its checks (SES-02, SES-04).
 4. The system records evidence: answers, time, hints, repeated errors (ASM-03, AI-08).
 5. The AI identifies a possible gap and proposes one next step (AI-03, AI-06, AI-07).
 6. Student receives the next step and tries a fresh question (AI-06, AI-09).
 7. The mastery estimate updates (AI-08).
 8. Teacher sees class-level patterns (ANA-01, ANA-02).
 9. Teacher decides an intervention: reteach, assign practice or move on (ANA-04).
 10. Parent receives an approved progress summary (PAR-03).
 11. The loop repeats with the next lesson.

Worked example (Class 9 Physics, Motion, illustrative)
• 10:00 Teacher teaches Motion. Lesson recording is uploaded (LES-07).
• 14:00 Teacher publishes notes and creates Session 3, opening at 18:00.
• 18:30 Student A scores 5 of 5 and receives a harder Speed and Velocity set. Student B scores 2 of 5 on
  Acceleration and receives a simpler explanation with worked examples. Student C missed the lesson and
  receives a catch-up path.
• Next morning Teacher sees that most of the class is stuck on Acceleration and spends 10 minutes reteaching it.
• Evening Parent sees that Session 3 is complete, the quiz result and one suggested next step. The parent does
  not see Student B's private AI chat.
Note: names, scores and times are fictional and illustrate the workflow only.

* Let's learn!              (waving, sparkle lines) Start of a session or greeting.

     * You got this!        (happy, closed eyes)            Encouragement after progress.
     * Hmm...               (question mark)                 Thinking or confusion support.

     * Great job!           (small heart)                   Meaningful milestone.
     * Time to rest         (sleeping, "zZ")                End of day or resting state.


Mascot behaviour rules (from S2 and S3):
 1. Idle: slow, subtle floating motion only.
 2. Greeting: short welcome when the student opens the learning space.
 3. Thinking: restrained motion while the AI works.
 4. Encouragement: small reaction after meaningful progress.
 5. Confusion: appears when the student asks for help or repeatedly gets stuck.
 6. Celebration: brief, only after a real milestone.
 7. Resting: unobtrusive while the student reads or solves.
 8. Error: says something failed, never blames the student.
 9. Reduced motion: static image when the device requests reduced motion.
 10. Consistency: the same recognisable character everywhere.
The mascot must never flash constantly, interrupt a lesson, or use emotional pressure to keep a student online. It
does not control educational logic.

     05             User roles and permissions


5.1 Roles
Student                     Studies, practises, asks the AI, views own progress.
Teacher                     Manages assigned classes and subjects, publishes material, creates sessions and
                            homework, reviews results.
Class teacher               A teacher with extra rights for one class: class updates, class space management.
Parent                      Follows one or more verified children. Sees approved progress, not private AI chats.
School admin                Manages the school's structure: classes, sections, subjects, teacher assignments,
                            enrolment and parent links.
Platform admin              Runs the platform itself: security, service health and authorised support. Separate from
                            the school admin.


5.2 Permission principles
• Access is enforced on the server and in the database, never only hidden in the interface (see SEC-01).
• Changing a role label in the browser never grants access.
• A user sees only their school, their classes, their children or their own records.
• Teachers cannot see classes they are not assigned to.
• Parents cannot see children they are not linked to.
• Students cannot open teacher or admin pages by changing the URL.
• School admins manage structure. They do not see individual AI chats and do not rank students publicly.

     13             AI learning engine (core)

The AI is the personalisation layer. Its core functions are: answer questions in the school's context, explain in more
than one way, find the likely gap behind a wrong answer, and suggest one next step. Every AI feature follows the
grounding and review rules in Section 25.
Testing rule (from S4): "Could a general chatbot do this on its own?" If yes, the feature is not a differentiator. The
differentiators are the school's notes, the class's quiz results, the curriculum position and the student's learning
history. Those exist only inside the platform.

   AI-01             Context-grounded Ask Saathy                                                            CORE


   Input the AI receives: the student's class, board, subject, chapter, current lesson, relevant teacher notes, and
   recent quiz evidence where it helps.
   Example: the student types "Explain acceleration." The AI answers in the teacher's notation and ends with
   "This comes from your teacher's notes, Motion, slide 4."
   Rules:
    • If the school material does not cover the question, the AI says it is giving a general explanation.
    • The AI never says "your teacher said" unless the material shows it.
    • Every answer ends with a short check for understanding.
   Failure: if the AI service is unavailable, the screen shows the saved notes for that concept and a retry button
   (Section 27).



   AI-02             Context retrieval                                                                      CORE


   Before answering, the system selects the approved material for the student's class and concept. Only that
   material plus the question is sent to the AI service. The selection uses the concept tags from LES-03. Full
   search across all uploads is an advanced upgrade (AI-30).



   AI-03             Concept and prerequisite graph                                                         CORE


   The platform stores which concepts depend on which. Example chain:
   Distance > Speed and Velocity > Acceleration > Graphs of motion.
   Teachers can review and correct these links.



   AI-04             Mistake explanations                                                                   CORE


   When a student answers wrongly, the system explains the likely reason in two or three sentences and shows
   the relevant slide or note. The student can ask for another explanation (AI-05).



   AI-05             Personalised explanation modes                                                         CORE


   The student can ask for the same concept in another form:
    • Simple language.
    • Step-by-step.
    • Everyday analogy.
    • Worked example.

Learn . Grow . Together                                                                                             Page 32
VIDHYASAATHY         Product Blueprint and Application Specification                                    Working draft v1




    • Formal textbook wording (the form used in exams).
    • Bilingual or Hinglish explanation (IND-03).
   After each explanation the system checks whether understanding improved.
   If the student is still stuck after two attempts, the system suggests teacher help rather than repeating the same
   answer.



   AI-06             Personalised study plan                                                            CORE


   Trigger: the student says what is hard, for example "I am not able to understand Coordinate Geometry."
   Steps the plan can include:
    1. Revise the prerequisite if the evidence shows a gap (AI-07).
    2. Read the teacher's note for the concept.
    3. Watch a recording segment or curated video.
    4. Study one worked example.
    5. Solve three easy questions.
    6. Take a five-question mini check.
    7. Move to application questions if the check passes; repeat step 2 or 3 if it does not.
   Each step links to a real school resource. The plan changes after each result. Teacher-assigned work stays
   visible and takes priority.



   AI-07             Prerequisite gap detector                                                          CORE


   Logic, in plain words: when a student gets a later concept wrong, the system checks the earlier concepts on the
   chain.
    • If the earlier concept is solid, the gap is in the later concept.
   Recommend revision of the later concept.
    • If the earlier concept is weak, move one step back and check again.
    • Stop at the first weak link and recommend fixing it, for example: "Acceleration needs velocity first. Spend 10

   Guardrail: if there is not enough evidence (for example, velocity was never practised), the system says so and
   recommends practice or a teacher question. It does not guess.
   Minimum evidence before a prerequisite gap is suggested: [OPEN] (Decision 4).

The following features address conditions the sources describe for Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities and schools. The sources

     25             Security, privacy and responsible AI


25.1 Security basics (SEC-01 to SEC-04) [CORE]
SEC-01 Server-side permission checks on every request. Client-side role labels never grant access.
SEC-02 Secure password storage, time-limited reset links, rate limits on sign-in and activation attempts (AUTH-03,
AUTH-04).
SEC-03 Secure transport for all traffic. Credentials are never placed in browser-visible code. API keys stay on the
server.
SEC-04 Error messages reveal no account details. Logs contain no personal student data.


25.2 Data minimisation and retention (SEC-05) [CORE]
Collect only what each feature needs. Define retention periods for learning history, AI interaction logs and
notifications [OPEN: Decision 11]. Support account correction and deletion requests through the school.


25.3 Parent-student access (SEC-06) [CORE]
A parent sees only verified, linked children. Link changes are logged (SCH-08, SCH-14).


25.4 Child-safety and age-appropriate interaction (SEC-07) [CORE]
• Ask Saathy answers within school subjects and uses age-appropriate language.
• Off-topic or unsafe questions are redirected politely and, where the content concerns a student's wellbeing,
  passed to a teacher or a trusted adult, not answered as a learning question.
• The product does not offer advice on self-harm, substances or other sensitive personal topics; it directs the
  student to a trusted adult.
Note: the exact escalation process and staffing for this are a school and policy decision [OPEN].


25.5 Responsible AI (SEC-08) [CORE]
• Ground school answers in approved material where possible (AI-01, AI-02).
• Show uncertainty. Never invent citations, page numbers or teacher remarks.
• Keep AI drafts separate from published content. Teacher approval is required for any content that reaches
  students.
• Avoid medical or psychological diagnosis and permanent labels.
• Treat instructions inside uploaded files as untrusted text, not as commands to the AI (prompt injection defence).
• Provide a fallback when the AI service fails (Section 27).
• Deterministic calculations for numerical simulations (AI-26).


25.6 Evidence integrity (SEC-09) [CORE]
Keep these separate in the database and in every display:

Activity completed          the student opened or finished something

     29             Acceptance criteria checklist

Accounts and permissions
[ ] A student cannot open a teacher or admin page by changing the URL.
[ ] A parent sees only children linked and verified to them.
[ ] A teacher sees only assigned classes and student work.
[ ] Signing out prevents access to protected pages.
[ ] Failed sign-in or activation does not reveal account details.
[ ] A roll number alone never signs a student in.

Learning workflow
[ ] A teacher can publish notes to the intended class only.
[ ] An authorised student can open a published session at its release time.
[ ] The student can answer and submit a supported quiz.
[ ] The system records the answer and its result.
[ ] The student sees an appropriate next step.
[ ] Completing a session requires a quiz or check attempt.
[ ] The teacher can view class-level results for an assigned class.
[ ] The parent sees only the permitted summary.
[ ] The app separates activity completed from concept demonstrated.

AI behaviour
[ ] Answers use approved school material where available.
[ ] Unsupported questions do not produce fabricated sources.
[ ] AI drafts stay unpublished until a teacher approves them.
[ ] Numerical simulations use deterministic calculations.
[ ] Unclear handwriting can be confirmed by the student before analysis.

     31             Glossary

Activation code One-time code a student uses to join their class account.
Adaptive practice Practice that changes difficulty based on recent answers.

Mastery estimate A working, uncertain estimate of understanding from evidence.
Prerequisite A concept that must be understood before another.
Question bank The school's library of approved questions.
Release time When a session becomes visible to students.
Socratic mode The AI guides with questions instead of giving the answer.

     32             Final product definition

VidhyaSaathy is a school-connected learning workspace for each class. It keeps the school's support working after
the school bell: teachers publish approved notes, recordings, guided sessions and homework; students follow a
clear next step and ask questions in the context of their own school's material; teachers see class-level patterns and
decide what to reteach; parents see approved, plain-language progress and one useful way to help.
Its foundation is not the number of AI features. It is the connection between school-approved content, student
evidence, a grounded next step and a teacher's decision. The cloud mascot gives the product a friendly face, the
guided session gives it structure, the AI gives it personalisation, the teacher heat map gives it insight, and the parent
summary gives it shared understanding.
Product principle: One classroom. Different learning paths. One connected support system.
