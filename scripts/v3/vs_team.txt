1. The idea in one minute
  VidhyaSaathy is an online copy of each school class. Whatever happens in the offline class
  (notes, homework, tests, lesson recordings) also appears online in that class's workspace, so the
  school keeps helping the student at home.


• One workspace per class, for example Class 9-A. Students, teachers and parents are connected to it.
• The teacher leads the class. Teachers publish notes, homework, recordings and self-study sessions.
• Self-study sessions are fixed. The school makes them. The teacher decides the day they open.
• An AI helper answers questions and builds a study plan that points to the right school note, recording
   or video.
• Parents see what the child is studying and what is done, not every private conversation.

The core loop

                       Notes and
                                           Student studies                         AI suggests next    Teacher sees
 Teacher teaches       session                                  Quiz / check
                                           and practises                           step                class gaps
                       published

Then the loop repeats the next day, with the teacher's next class.

The one-line thesis

  Why should the school's support end when the bell rings?

3. Where the idea came from
The founder was a strong student, scoring above 90% in earlier classes. In Class 9, studying became
chaotic. There was no coaching, so when a topic like Biology or Physics was hard, the founder searched
YouTube, asked friends for notes, and used AI tools. Friends' "perfect notes" were all different, and the
founder had to decide what to trust.

  Lesson from this: the problem was not that nothing was available. It was that nothing came
  together into one coherent path. This is one student's experience, not proof that every student faces
  the same problem.

5. How a school joins
1. School is registered on VidhyaSaathy.
2. Classes and sections are created (for example 9-A and 9-B).
3. Teachers are assigned to their subject and class.
4. Students are added to their class.
5. Parents are linked to their verified child.
6. Syllabus, chapters and notes are organised by subject.
7. Each class workspace goes live. Notes from day one can be published.

  OPEN: Who adds the student list? The school admin uploads it, the class teacher adds students, or
  students self-join with a code. Login needs more than a roll number, because a roll number alone is
  not secure. Options: school-issued activation code, verified parent phone, or admin approval.

Example: a self-study session

  Self-Study Session 3, Physics, Motion (fictional example)
  1. Learning goal: understand distance and displacement.
  2. Slide 1: definition, one idea only. Slide 2: diagram. Slide 3: worked example.
  3. Classroom recording for the same topic (optional).
  4. Five short MCQs.
  5. Recap and "next step": Speed and Velocity.



Example: AI study plan
Student says: "I am not able to understand Coordinate Geometry." The plan (proposed) could look like
this:
• Step 1: Revise the coordinate basics note from school.
• Step 2: Watch the 6-minute classroom recording segment on plotting points.
• Step 3: Study one worked example.
• Step 4: Solve 3 easy questions.
• Step 5: Take a 5-question mini-check.
• Step 6: Move to application questions if the check is good.

7. The teacher experience
• Less copying. Notes are published once and reused each year. Students do not spend class time
  copying.
• Sessions are reusable. Teacher can change questions per class. The session structure stays fixed.
• Timed release. "Complete Self-Study Session 3 tonight." The session opens on the chosen day.
• Quiz as a check. Teacher sees who completed the work and who did not.
• Class-level view. Teacher sees which concept many students missed (for example Acceleration).

  Principle: one teaching effort, many uses. Notes + recording + session + quiz from the same lesson.
  Teachers stay in control of content and assessment.


8. Personalised study plans
The AI does not just answer. It asks what the student needs next, based on the class work and the
student's results.

Four internal performance bands (PROPOSED)
 Band                    Internal meaning (never shown as a                Possible support
                         label)

 0 to 25%                Large gaps                                        Simpler explanation, prerequisite revision

 25 to 50%               Basics partly clear                               Worked examples, guided practice

 50 to 75%               Basics clear                                      Application questions, more practice

 75 to 100%              Consistent understanding                          Next concept, harder challenges


  Team caution: students must not be called weak, average or smart. A single overall percentage
  hides which concept is weak. Tracking per concept is probably better, and the thresholds are not
  validated. OPEN.


Different ways to learn (PROPOSED)
Some students learn by practising, some by listening, some by visuals, and some by games. VidhyaSaathy
can offer all of these for the same topic. It should not lock a student into one "learning style", because
strong evidence for fixed learning styles does not exist.


9. Games and formats
Lightweight games tied to the chapter. Illustrative list, not final. The founder's idea is 4 to 5 games that
change slightly for each chapter.

 Game format                            How it works                                      Helps with

 Timed concept challenge                Answer quick questions before the timer ends      Recall of definitions

 MCQ rush                               Chapter MCQs with streaks                         Practice and speed

 Match the concept                      Match terms to definitions or formulas            Vocabulary and formulas

 Quick revision quiz                    Five random questions from the chapter            Revision before a test

 Chapter progress challenge             Unlock the next level after mastering a concept   Sequential progress

Rule: games must reward understanding and correct practice, not just time spent on screen.
