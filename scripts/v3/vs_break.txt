  PA R T 1 · F O U N D AT I O N


Product Foundation
What VidhyaSaathy is, the single loop every feature orbits, the problem it solves, the principles that
govern every design decision, and the boundary between what the product is and is not.

  1.1 What VidhyaSaathy Is
VidhyaSaathy is a school-connected learning platform — an online copy of each school class.
Whatever happens in the offline classroom (notes, homework, tests, lesson recordings) also appears
online in that class’s workspace, so the school keeps helping the student at home. It is formally
defined in the Blueprint as an AI-powered School-to-Home Learning Continuity Platform.
A school already provides teachers, textbooks, explanations, notes, assignments, tests and guidance
— but once students return home, they must independently decide what to study, which resources to
trust, how to understand difficult topics and how to recover when they fall behind. Students switch
between YouTube videos, PDFs, friends’ notes, websites and separate AI tools without knowing which
explanation matches their school’s teaching. VidhyaSaathy connects these disconnected activities
into one system:
   One workspace per class (for example Class 9-A). Students, teachers and parents are all
   connected to it.
   The teacher leads the class: they publish notes, homework, recordings and guided self-study
   sessions.
   Self-study sessions are fixed, structured units. The school makes them available; the teacher
   decides the day they open.
   An AI helper answers questions in the context of the subject, class and the teacher’s own material,
   and builds a study plan that points to the right school note, recording or video.
   Parents see what the child is studying and what is completed — not every private conversation.




                                                               1
  Te one-line thesis of the whole product: “Why should the school’s support end when the bell rings?” —
  the school speaks again after the bell rings.

Students should be able to answer four questions at any moment: What should I study today? What
do I still not understand? How do I practise it? What should I do next?

  it uses reliable evidence from previous sessions.

  1.4 Where the Idea Came From
The founder was a strong student, scoring above 90% in earlier classes. In Class 9, studying became
chaotic. There was no coaching, so when a topic like Biology or Physics was hard, the founder
searched YouTube, asked friends for notes, and used AI tools. Friends’ “perfect notes” were all
different, and the founder had to decide what to trust. The lesson carried into the product: the
problem was not that nothing was available — it was that nothing came together into one coherent
path. This is one student’s experience, not proof that every student faces the same problem, which is
why the sources mark market claims as hypotheses to validate. The first target is schools in Indian
Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities, as an initial interest rather than a validated market.

  1.8 Feature IDs, Statuses and the Long-Term Moat
Feature IDs. The Master Working Spec (S4) assigns every feature a permanent ID from VS-01 to
 VS-100 so the team can discuss “How is VS-42?” without ambiguity. The spec describes the
catalogue as 101 features (the numbering plus the core loop). This document preserves those IDs
exactly and adds Blueprint-only modules with their PRD citations (Section 17.3).
Statuses. Source documents mark commitment levels: CONFIRMED items are part of the idea as the
founder described it; PROPOSED items are specifications to discuss; OPEN items are explicitly
undecided. This document shows them as CORE , PROPOSED and OPEN , plus build-phase tags P1–
P4 from the build order (Part 15).

Long-term moat (S5 §79). No individual feature guarantees a moat. The defensible advantage may
emerge from the combination of: (1) school-specific content and curriculum mapping, (2) teacher
workflows and adoption, (3) longitudinal, evidence-backed concept histories, (4) misconception
patterns connected to actual student responses, (5) intervention history and follow-up outcomes, (6)
low-friction school onboarding, (7) parent-teacher-student continuity, and (8) reliable performance on
ordinary devices and constrained connections. These are hypotheses to validate through real use —
not advantages that can be claimed before implementation.

  1.9 Foundation Checklist

    No feature labels students (weak / average / smart) anywhere in the UI

    Claims about markets and outcomes stay marked as hypotheses until validated (Section 16.5)

Brand, Theme and the Cloud Companion
The selected direction, confirmed by the brand board (S3): a friendly cloud mascot hugging a book —
soft, modern, approachable. This is the visual language of every screen in the app.

  2.1 Brand Identity and Mascot
The mascot is a cute blue cloud hugging a book and reading, sitting on stacks of books in the poster
art, under the message “Small steps make big progress.” The brand must feel:
   Trustworthy enough for schools and parents.
   Friendly enough for students to enjoy using daily.
   Modern enough to feel like a new AI learning product.
   Calm rather than visually noisy.
   Distinctive without copying Duolingo, Chikoo or another existing brand.
Tagline options discussed in the sources (one must be chosen — Section 17.4): “Learn • Grow •
Together.” (shown on the brand board) · “Grow at your own pace.” · “Your school, online, after the bell
rings.” (the product one-liner).

  2.3 Interface Rules

≈80% clean, functional interface   ≈15% soft colour   ≈5% playful mascot & illustration

   Use blue for primary actions and important learning states; peach for supportive highlights and
   celebrations; warm white for comfortable reading; deep navy for text.
   Avoid purple/neon-heavy styling, overly cartoonish interfaces and excessive gradients.
   Teacher dashboards may be more information-dense than student screens while preserving the
   same visual identity.
   Use accessible contrast, readable typography, clear focus states and responsive layouts.

  2.4 Logo Requirements
The final logo must have: (1) a recognisable cloud mascot or cloud-based mark — the “V” cloud mark
on the brand board is the working direction; (2) a wordmark reading “VidhyaSaathy”; (3) a
standalone icon for app favicons and small screens; (4) a monochrome version; (5) a version that
works on light and dark backgrounds; (6) a simplified version that remains legible at small sizes. The
exact final artwork, font and tagline remain to be approved (Open — Section 17.4). The team mockup
is titled ClassOS and the product name itself is still OPEN — the idea matters more than the name.

Entry System: Sign-Up, Sign-In, Onboarding
How every user — school, admin, teacher, student, parent — gets into the system. The school’s existing
structure (classes, roll numbers, staff IDs) is the app’s backbone: the app is a mirror of the school, not a
new system.

 V S - 01 – V S - 08

  3.1 Public Landing Page
The landing page explains what VidhyaSaathy does before asking anyone to create an account. Its
sections (S5 §7): (1) a hero explaining the school-to-home learning promise; (2) student, teacher,
parent and school use cases; (3) a visual explanation of the learning loop; (4) a preview of guided
learning sessions; (5) a preview of AI-assisted learning and teacher insights; (6) how school material
supports AI responses; (7) privacy and child-safety information; (8) school onboarding or contact
option; (9) sign-in and account-access links.

  G UA R DR A IL
  The landing page must not claim proven learning improvements, existing school partnerships, or
  advanced capabilities that have not been validated.


   VS-01      School Onboarding Wizard                                                        CORE     P1

  The complete setup that brings a school onto the platform — an 8-step wizard run by the school
  admin:

                                                       8
 1   Admin registers the school on VidhyaSaathy.
 2   School profile — name, board (CBSE / ICSE / State), city.
 3   Academic year is set.
 4   Classes and sections are created (e.g. 9-A and 9-B).
 5   Subjects are added.

 VS-02    School Admin Account & Login                                                       CORE    P1

The admin is the school’s owner account, created during registration. Login with staff ID/email +
password; two-step verification optional. The admin sees the full school structure and NO student
marks — “structure, not surveillance.” Admin capabilities are documented in Part 9 ( VS-79 to VS-
87 ).
The Blueprint’s school registration adds: an authorised representative starts registration → the platform
verifies the representative → the school profile is created → admins set up classes → teachers are
invited → students are created/invited → parents are linked → subjects configured → first material
published (S5 §8).


 VS-03    Teacher Account & Login                                                            CORE    P1

 1   The admin assigns the teacher (subject + class).
 2   The teacher receives a staff ID + temporary password.
 3   On first login the teacher sets a PIN/password and completes the profile.
 4   The dashboard shows ONLY that teacher’s assigned classes.
 5   Class-teacher role = extra rights for one class (updates, class-space management — VS-72 ).

Creation flow (S5 §10): admin invites or creates the account → teacher verifies invitation → signs in →
completes profile → sees assigned classes and subjects → confirms teaching assignments → can
upload/create material for authorised classes → sees a short setup guide for the first guided session.
Teachers must never automatically see unrelated classes or student records outside their
responsibilities.




                                                    9
 VS-04    Student Verification & Login — critical security flow                       CORE     P1      ★


The principle: students are IDENTIFIED by roll number, but roll number alone is NEVER the login. Roll
numbers are public — the whole class knows them. This is children’s data; this layer cannot be
skipped.
 1   Student opens the app → “Select your school.”
 2   Enters roll/admission number.
 3   System finds the record but does not let the user in yet.
 4   System asks for the school-issued activation code (one-time use).
 5   An OTP is sent to the parent’s registered phone number.
 6   Both must match.
 7   Student sets a personal 4-digit PIN.
 8   On every later login: school + roll number + PIN only.

Fallback: if the parent’s OTP cannot arrive (feature-phone offline, etc.), the admin manually approves
the student. Why it matters: a student should never see records merely because someone knows a
roll number — and registration must not allow an arbitrary user to join a private school class just by
knowing its name (S5 §9). Which exact verification combination is default remains OPEN (Section 17.4).


 VS-05    Parent Account Linking                                                             CORE   P1

 1   The student CSV already carries the parent’s phone number.
 2   When the student verifies ( VS-04 ), the OTP goes to exactly that number.
 3   The parent receives an automatic message: “Your child has joined VidhyaSaathy. Download the
     app and log in with this number.”
 4   The parent creates their account with OTP verification.
 5   The parent sees data for ONLY their verified children.
 6   Two children? Both are linked; one account, quick switch.

Hard rule: a parent must not be able to connect to a child simply by entering a name or searching a
public directory. Guardian cases (uncle, grandfather, etc. — common in India) are handled by VS-84 .


 VS-06    PIN-Based Shared Device Profiles                                            PROPOSED      P3

Multiple profiles on ONE phone — because in India one phone often serves the whole family. On
opening the app, a “Who are you?” screen lists the profiles. Each profile has its own PIN; switching
takes two taps. If the phone is taken away mid-session, progress is saved — “continue from where
you left off.”
Cross-ref: connects to VS-88 (offline sync must respect per-profile data) and the shared-device
sign-out reminders in Section 3.10.




                                                   10
    VS-07       Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)                                                CORE   P1

  One platform, four doors — each role sees only its own work:
    Role               Sees / can do

      Student          Own class workspace, own study plan, own progress.
      Teacher          Only assigned classes; subject teachers only their subject workspace.
      Parent           Only their verified child’s permitted data.
      Admin            School structure only — never student ranking.

  Principle: privacy’s first door is BLOCKED IN THE DESIGN, not patched later. Enforcement must be
  server-side (Section 3.10 rule 10; Part 13.1). Changing a client-side role label must never grant
  additional access.


    VS-08       Board-Wise Curriculum Mapping                                                   CORE   P1

  During onboarding the school selects its board (CBSE / ICSE / State). The platform provides that
  board’s chapter-concept structure ready-made (NCERT listings are public data), so teachers never
  build structure from zero. “Motion” exists in both CBSE and UP Board syllabi, but at different depths.
  This mapping is the product’s lifeblood — it makes tagging ( VS-11 ), gap detection ( VS-30 ) and
  syllabus GPS ( VS-58 ) possible.

  3.10 Sign-In and Session Management Rules
Required behaviours for ALL roles (S5 §12):
 1. Email, username or school-issued identifier may be used, according to the chosen authentication
    configuration.
 2. Password-based authentication where appropriate (plus PINs per VS-04 / VS-06 for students).
 3. Secure account recovery must be available.
 4. Users are routed to their authorised dashboard after authentication.
 5. Student accounts must not be able to open teacher or admin routes by changing a URL.
 6. Expired sessions return users to a secure sign-in state.
 7. Sign-out must end the relevant session.
 8. Error messages explain the issue WITHOUT revealing sensitive account information.
 9. Shared-device users are reminded to sign out when appropriate.
10. Authentication and permissions are enforced on the SERVER, not only hidden in the interface.

   CR I T I CA L
   The role selected on a login screen is never treated as proof that the user has that role.

   VS-32       AI Honesty Layer — rules above every AI feature                                        CORE

   1. CITE THE SOURCE: answers distinguish “from your teacher’s notes” vs “general explanation.”

 VS-41      Rote-Learning Detector (“Ratta Detector”)                                           PROPOSED         ★


India’s biggest silent learning disease: definition perfect, marks arrive, application = zero. Detection

application 1/5 — this looks like rote learning. Let’s reopen the concept, 7 minutes.” Teacher view: “14
students have perfect definitions; 9 have weak application — this is the ‘everyone understood’ illusion

     6.10 AI Request Workflow, Governance and Safety Rules
A typical school-related AI request follows this server-side sequence (S5 §93):
 1     Authenticate the user.
 2     Verify the user’s permissions.
 3     Identify the relevant school, class, subject and concept.
 4     Retrieve relevant approved resources.
 5     Include only the necessary learning context.
 6     Send the request to the configured AI service.
 7     Validate the returned structure and content where possible.
 8     Attach source references when supported.
 9     Save only the information required for the learning workflow.
10     Return the response to the user.
11     Record the educational outcome if a follow-up activity is completed.

AI responses are never trusted merely because they are well written.

     A I S A FETY R UL ES (S 5 § 8 3 )
     Ground school answers in approved sources where possible · show uncertainty when evidence is
     insufficient · never fabricate citations · keep AI suggestions visually separate from teacher-approved
     content · no medical or psychological diagnoses, no permanent student labels · safe, age-appropriate
     explanations · protect student data from unnecessary disclosure · treat prompt injection inside uploaded
     documents as untrusted input · provide a fallback whenever the AI service is unavailable.

  13.2 Student Data Protection (S5 §82)
The system should: collect only necessary information; protect account credentials; restrict access to

sensitive administrative actions where appropriate; provide processes for account correction and

The implementation must follow applicable Indian privacy and child-data requirements, with

    15.2 Blueprint Build Priorities (S5 Part 16)
    Phase 1 — six-hour hackathon prototype: one complete, convincing learning loop, not many
    disconnected screens — polished landing/sign-in; seeded demo school and Class 9-A; three role
    dashboards; a published Motion lesson; one guided session; a short diagnostic quiz; two seeded
    student profiles with different results; a personalised explanation or recovery path; a simple
    concept-level teacher dashboard; a parent-friendly progress summary; a working database
    connection where feasible; a deterministic AI fallback; a polished cloud mascot and consistent
    theme; a tested end-to-end demo. Sample student data must be clearly labelled as simulated —
    no claims of improved real outcomes.
    Phase 2 — functional school pilot: real authentication; school/class setup; secure teacher/student
    permissions; resource uploads; lesson creation and publishing; guided session scheduling;
    assignments and assessments; persisted responses; basic parent-student linking; teacher-
    reviewed AI content; reliable progress tracking; basic admin tools.
    Phase 3 — intelligent learning system: concept/prerequisite graph; learning-state model;
    misconception detection; Learning Recovery Engine; adaptive practice; AI-generated lesson
    packages; classroom concept radar; reteaching assistant; evidence-based parent summaries;
    Learning Passport.
    Phase 4 — signature AI experiences: What-If Machine; Mistake Time Machine with supported
    handwriting analysis; Chapter-to-Comic Studio; Infinite Textbook Adventure; Concept Court;
    Reverse Exam Mode; Viva Simulator; Reality Scanner; Learning Remix; AI Debate. Prioritise by user
    testing, educational value, cost and reliability — not impressiveness.
    Phase 5 — scale and school adoption: bulk onboarding; more grades and curricula; additional
    Indian languages; improved low-bandwidth behaviour; optional offline learning and sync; school-
    to-school portability of permitted records; more sophisticated analytics; practical integrations with
    existing school systems.

Acceptance Criteria and QA Checklists
The master checklists from the Blueprint (S5 Part 17) — the definition of “working” for this product.

  16.1 Account and Permission Tests
    A student cannot open teacher-only functions by changing the URL.
    A parent can see only the child or children correctly linked.
    A teacher can access only authorised classes and student work.
    Signing out prevents access to protected pages.
    Failed authentication does not reveal sensitive account information.

  16.2 Learning Workflow Tests
    A teacher can publish a lesson to the intended class.
    An authorised student can open the lesson.
    The student can submit a supported answer.
    The system records the answer and its result.
    The student receives the appropriate next step.
    The teacher can view authorised class-level results.
    The parent sees the permitted progress summary.
    The app distinguishes activity completion from demonstrated mastery.

  16.3 AI Reliability Tests
    AI explanations use relevant approved content where available.
    Unsupported questions never trigger fabricated source claims.
    AI-generated lesson drafts are reviewable before publication.

                                                       49
      Numerical simulations use deterministic calculations.
      Uncertain handwriting/solution parsing is confirmed by the student.
      The system handles AI failure without losing completed work.

  16.4 User Experience Tests
      The primary student action is easy to find.
      The teacher can publish a simple session without unnecessary steps.
      The parent understands the progress report.
      The interface works on common phone and laptop screen sizes.
      Text remains readable and actions have clear feedback.
      The cloud companion does not obstruct learning.
      Errors explain what happened and what the user can do next.

  16.5 Product Validation Tests — before claiming improved learning
      Can students complete a guided session independently?
      Does a personalised explanation improve performance on a fresh question?
      Do teachers save time compared with their existing workflow?
      Do teachers trust the generated content after review?
      Do parents understand the progress summaries?
      Does the product work reliably on the devices and connections students actually use?
      Does the learning loop produce better outcomes than simply sharing a PDF or video?

  18.3 Final Product Definition (S5 Closing)
VidhyaSaathy is an AI-powered, school-connected learning platform that helps students continue
learning at home, gives teachers actionable insight into student understanding, and helps parents
support progress through clear, evidence-based updates. Its foundation is not the number of AI
features. It is the connection between school-approved knowledge, student learning evidence,
personalised next steps and teacher intervention.
The cloud companion provides a friendly identity. The guided self-study system provides structure.
The AI learning engine provides personalisation. The teacher dashboard provides actionable insight.
The parent dashboard provides shared understanding. The long-term ambition: a student does not
merely receive an answer, but gets help identifying what they do not understand, learning it in a
suitable way, checking whether they improved, and continuing with the right next step.

  PRODUCT PRINCIPLE: One classroom. Different learning paths. One connected support system.
