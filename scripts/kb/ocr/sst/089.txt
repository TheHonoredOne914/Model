In modern democracies, heads of government exercise power
but are bound by constitutions and parliaments, which function
as representative bodies that check the powers of elected
leaders. Do the roles of the raja and the assemblies in Vedic
times suggest a similar principle? Can we say that systems of
governance in Indian history—since their early institutionalised
forms—were guided by the idea of ensuring people’s participation
in the political process?
