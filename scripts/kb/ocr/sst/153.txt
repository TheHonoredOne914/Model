Counting and Transfer of Votes

1. Voters mark their preference.

2. First preference votes are counted for all candidates. Those
who receive the minimum votes required to win (quota) are
declared elected.

3. The candidate who receives the lowest votes is eliminated.
Their votes are transferred to those who are mentioned as
second preference on those ballot papers.

4. Process continues till the required number of candidates are
elected.

The Laws

Let us now briefly examine the major legislations that govern the
conduct of elections in India—The Representation of the People
Act, 1950, and the Representation of the People Act, 1951. There
also exists ‘The Presidential and Vice-Presidential Act, 1952’. The
Representation of the People Act, 1950 deals mainly with the
allocation of seats and delimitation of constituencies, the preparation
and revision of electoral rolls, and ensuring that every citizen
above 18 years of age has the right to vote without discrimination.
The Representation of the People Act, 1951 deals with all the other
aspects of the conduct of elections and post-election disputes. It lays
down rules for nomination of candidates, election campaigns,
voting procedures, and resolution of disputes. Together, these Acts
ensure the integrity, transparency, and accountability of India’s
electoral process.
