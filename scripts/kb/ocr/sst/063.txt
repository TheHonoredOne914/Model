Since these sites show a cultural continuity, this stage is generally
described as Early Harappan. We see continuity in pottery traditions,
semi-precious stone beads, shell bangles, terracotta objects, and
copper working. Some other developments, such as building a
perimeter wall around the settlement, the use of seals, and even
the Harappan writings/script, probably had their beginnings in the
Early Harappan stage.

The Harappans were not merely agriculturists; they also practised
a number of arts and crafts, and pottery was one of the major craft
products that boosted their economy. Pottery remains from different
regions show unique styles in the shapes of vessels and their painted
designs. Some other crafts that show gradual development include
copper work, shell work, and the production of semi-precious stone
beads. Evidence of Early Harappan bead production is found from
Harappa in Pakistan as well as from Kunal in Haryana and Datrana
in Gujarat.
