11.5.5 What happens when a sperm meets an egg?

At birth, a girl's ovaries already have millions of immature eggs. From
puberty onwards, usually one mature egg is released every month from
one of the ovaries. This is called ovulation. Before ovulation, the uterus
starts to prepare itself; the inner lining becomes thick. As the ovulation
happens, the egg travels from the ovary to the oviduct. During sexual
intercourse, millions of sperm enter through the vagina. They swim
through the reproductive tract and may reach the egg in
the oviduct. If a sperm encounters an egg and succeeds
in fusing with it, a zygote is formed (Fig. 11.20), The
inner lining of the uterus becomes thicker and richer in
blood vessels. The zygote undergoes a series of mitotic
divisions while travelling to the uterus, and implants into
the inner lining of the uterus to receive nourishment for
development. This implantation marks the beginning
of pregnancy.
