In the Himalayan region, water flowing downhill converts its
potential energy into kinetic energy. Traditionally, this energy
was used in devices, such as the gharat or panchakki—a water
mill used to grind grain (Fig. 7.36). These can still be found in
hilly regions.

The water starts from the top with potential energy. This
potential energy gets canverted to kinetic energy as it comes
down the pipe (A). The kinetic energy of the water drives
the wheel (B) and sets it into rotational motion. The wheel is
connected to the grinding stone at the top (C).

In modern times, the potential energy of the water stored Fig. 7.36: A watermill
in dams is similarly converted into kinetic energy to generate (gharat or panchakki)
electricity.
