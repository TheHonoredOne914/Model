5.4.3 Suspensions

Let us look closely at the container shown in Fig. 5.2. No matter how well
you stir, the sand particles can be seen clearly, unlike in a solution, where
the solute particles are not visible. Such heterogeneous mixtures in which
the solid particles (sand) do not dissolve but remain suspended throughout
the bulk of the medium (water) are called suspensions. In the case of a
suspension, the particles of the undissolved substance are larger in size
than the particles present in the solution. Particles of a suspension are
visible to the naked eye. Some other examples of suspension are sawdust
suspended in water and tea leaves in water.

How can we separate mud from water?

If you leave a container of muddy water undisturbed, the heavier mud
particles settle at the bottom and the water may still appear cloudy. You can
filter the muddy water through a cotton cloth or filter paper. While some
larger particles will be removed, the water often remains cloudy. This shows
that filtration is not always enough to separate tiny particles. If the muddy
water is still not clear even after keeping for some time, how can it be cleaned?
In such cases, we use techniques, such as centrifugation and/or coagulation.
