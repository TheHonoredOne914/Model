8.7 How Are Electrons Distributed in Different
Energy Levels?
Bohr and Bury suggested the following rules:
* The maximum number of electrons present in a shell is given by the
formula 2n?, where ‘n’ is the number of the shell (Fig. 8.7). Hence, K-shell

(n = 1) 212 = 2, can accommodate 2 electrons, L-shell (n = 2) 2x22 =8

electrons, and M-shell (n = 3) 2*3? = 18 electrons.

© The maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the
outermost shell is 8 (the first shell can accommodate a maximum of two
electrons).

© Electrons are filled in these shells in a stepwise manner, starting from

the one closest to the nucleus and moving outward, i.e., in the order K,

L, M,N, ... The L-shell will be filled only after the K-shell is complete, and

so on.

To understand the placement of electrons in atoms, let us begin with
